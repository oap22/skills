## Part 1 — Dictated samples (Wispr Flow, 2026-09-20)

Collected one prompt at a time, unedited. Transcription quirks left in.

### 1. Explaining his research to a non-CS friend

> I'll just talk about the research I'm doing currently, not my research group. The research I'm doing is with a physics professor who is a cosmologist. He does cosmology, which is the study of really large things like galaxies and space and that kind of thing.
>
> Essentially, what we're doing is taking telescope images and satellite images of clusters of galaxies (essentially, a group of galaxies that are all together) and then trying to weigh them. One of the issues that you have when you weigh them is that sometimes the galaxies overlap each other. This is called blending. That means that the galaxy cluster then overlaps another, and sometimes it's difficult to tell whether or not that galaxy is blended or not.
>
> What we're exploring is different machine learning algorithms that could potentially solve this problem more successfully than the current processes now.

Note: scopes the question before answering it. Glosses every term inline. Names the technical label *after* the plain description.

### 2. Same subject, to a professor he wants to work with

> Right now, the two different algorithms that we are working with are DeepDisc and Scarlet. These algorithms are focusing on using convolutional neural networks to pull apart the blended galaxy as well as locate the blended galaxy. Right now, the success rate isn't super high.
>
> What we are thinking is potentially using multiple different streams of data that have unique qualities. The data that we are trying to find will show a distinction or a difference between the galaxies that are blended and the galaxies that are not blended. This could mean being able to use different streams of data on top of the CNN, or refining the CNN so that it has a little bit more information about the galaxies, as well as potentially getting better data.
>
> Right now, we're waiting for a new sky survey that will give us some higher-fidelity images from a ground telescope with, obviously, all the different color filters as well.

Note: the shift from sample 1 is *specificity*, not formality. Contractions stay. "isn't super high" stays. Glossing disappears; proper nouns arrive undefined. He hedges *more* here, not less.

### 3. Disagreeing with a teammate's decision

> I don't think that would be the best direction for this group. I do appreciate your initiative there; however, I do think that next time you should reach out to me beforehand and discuss, especially if it's a big decision. We'll work from this, and it'll be okay. Next time, I would appreciate it if you would keep the group in on your thoughts before you make the decision.

Note: contractions drop. Emphatic *do* appears twice. Verdict → credit → correction → de-escalation → the ask again. He never argues the merits; he pivots to process.

### 5. A technical problem he solved

> Recently, I was having an issue with my Zed configuration and installing the Java SDK and the JavaFX kit. I spun up a couple of agents and had them look on Reddit, X, Stack Overflow, and anything they could find to get the config to work within Zed. Zed doesn't natively have a Java compiler, so we had to work through that.
>
> I worked through it with a team of agents, and eventually we found issues and were able to update the configs and get it working. We also then ported all of the work over from my C++ repo within C Lion and put it onto my Mac, which was super helpful. Now Zed is great.

Note: **the trap.** The entire debugging story compresses into "eventually we found issues and were able to update the configs." Also "we" for solo work done with agents, and a four-word closing verdict.

### 6. Telling a teammate he'll be late

> Hey teammate,
>
> Just wanted to let you know that I'm running into some blockers with X, and it's not going as I expected. I would love your support in the future. I'm guessing it will be done by Friday, probably by the end of the day. I'll have some agents look at it, but I'd probably want another set of eyes on it. If you could help me with that, it'd be great. I'll send you over some of the code, and then I'll check everything into the Git repository.
>
> Thanks. Sorry about that.

Note: contractions everywhere — he is at fault, not correcting. Bad news first, then a hedged date, then a plan, then the ask, then two words of apology.

### 7. Complaining

> I am actually getting so frustrated with having a bunch of different AI subscriptions and not being able to use them all in the same place. It has been so frustrating to have to jump around between apps and do a bunch of different things at once when I could just work within one app with one subscription with all the models that I want.
>
> Also, I'm getting extremely frustrated with the prices for compute. I literally cannot buy a computer right now. I have a MacBook that is great, but if I want to run any edge inference or I want to run any local models for a Jarvis, for example, or something like that, it's literally impossible. It is thousands of dollars to get started, and you don't even know how useful it would be until you get it.

Note: this is his ceiling. Repeats "frustrated" rather than escalating. Concedes ("I have a MacBook that is great, but"). Lands on the reasoned objection.

### 8. Ambition

> In 5 years, I would like to be working for Nvidia as a machine learning engineer within the healthcare space. I would like to do research for cancer detection models, for drug creation models, or something to do with helping people with diseases.

Note: terse about himself. "I would like to" twice. List of three with a soft third item. Plain words for the values statement.
